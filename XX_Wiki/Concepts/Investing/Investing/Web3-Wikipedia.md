**Web3** (also known as **Web 3.0**) [^1] [^2] [^3] is an idea for a new [iteration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iteration "Iteration") of the [World Wide Web](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Wide_Web "World Wide Web") which incorporates concepts such as [decentralization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decentralized_web "Decentralized web"), [blockchain technologies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blockchain "Blockchain"), and token-based economics.[^4] This is distinct from [Tim Berners-Lee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tim_Berners-Lee "Tim Berners-Lee") 's concept of the [Semantic Web](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semantic_Web "Semantic Web"). Some technologists and journalists have contrasted it with [Web 2.0](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Web_2.0 "Web 2.0"), in which they say [user-generated content](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/User-generated_content "User-generated content") is controlled by a small group of companies referred to as [Big Tech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Tech "Big Tech").[^5] The term "web3" was coined in 2014 by [Ethereum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethereum "Ethereum") co-founder [Gavin Wood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gavin_Wood "Gavin Wood"), and the idea gained interest in 2021 from [cryptocurrency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptocurrency "Cryptocurrency") enthusiasts, large technology companies, and [venture capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venture_capital "Venture capital") firms.[^5] [^6] The concepts of web3 were first represented in 2013.[^7] [^8]

Critics have expressed concerns over the [centralization of wealth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distribution_of_wealth "Distribution of wealth") to a small group of investors and individuals,[^9] or a loss of privacy due to more expansive data collection.[^10] Billionaires like [Elon Musk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elon_Musk "Elon Musk") and [Jack Dorsey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Dorsey "Jack Dorsey") have argued that web3 only serves as a [buzzword](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buzzword "Buzzword") or marketing term.[^11] [^12] [^13]

## Background

[Web 1.0](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Web_1.0 "Web 1.0") and [Web 2.0](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Web_2.0 "Web 2.0") refer to eras in the [history of the World Wide Web](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_World_Wide_Web "History of the World Wide Web") as it evolved through various technologies and formats. Web 1.0 refers roughly to the period from 1991 to 2004, where most sites consisted of [static pages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Static_web_page "Static web page"), and the vast majority of users were consumers, not producers of content.[^14] [^15] [Web 2.0](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Web_2.0 "Web 2.0") is based around the idea of "the web as platform" [^16] and centers on user-created content uploaded to [forums](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet_forum "Internet forum"), [social media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_media "Social media") and networking services, [blogs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blog "Blog"), and [wikis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wiki "Wiki"), among other services.[^17] Web 2.0 is generally considered to have begun around 2004 and continues to the current day.[^16] [^18] [^5]

## Terminology

Web3 is distinct from [Tim Berners-Lee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tim_Berners-Lee "Tim Berners-Lee") 's 1999 concept of a [Semantic Web](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semantic_Web "Semantic Web"), which was also sometimes referred to as Web 3.0.[^19] While the Semantic Web envisioned a web of linked data, web3 in the [blockchain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blockchain "Blockchain") context refers to a decentralized internet built upon distributed ledger technologies.[^19] Some writers referring to the decentralized concept usually known as "web3" have used the term "Web 3.0", leading to some confusion between the two concepts.[^20] [^21] Furthermore, some visions of web3 also incorporate ideas relating to the semantic web.[^22] [^23]

The term "web3" was coined by [Polkadot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polkadot_\(cryptocurrency\) "Polkadot (cryptocurrency)") founder and [Ethereum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethereum "Ethereum") co-founder [Gavin Wood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gavin_Wood "Gavin Wood") in 2014, referring to a "decentralized online ecosystem based on blockchain." [^1] In 2021, the idea of web3 gained popularity.[^24] Particular interest spiked toward the end of 2021, largely due to interest from [cryptocurrency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptocurrency "Cryptocurrency") enthusiasts and investments from high-profile technologists and companies.[^5] [^6] Executives from venture capital firm [Andreessen Horowitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andreessen_Horowitz "Andreessen Horowitz") traveled to [Washington, DC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington,_DC "Washington, DC"), in October 2021 to lobby for the idea as a potential solution to questions about regulation of the web, with which policymakers have been grappling.[^25]

## Concept

Specific visions for web3 differ, and the term has been described by Olga Kharif as "hazy", but they revolve around the idea of decentralization and often incorporate blockchain technologies, such as various [cryptocurrencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptocurrency "Cryptocurrency") and [non-fungible tokens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-fungible_token "Non-fungible token") (NFTs).[^5] Kharif has described web3 as an idea that "would build financial assets, in the form of tokens, into the inner workings of almost anything you do online".[^26] A policy brief published by the [Bennett Institute for Public Policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bennett_Institute_for_Public_Policy "Bennett Institute for Public Policy") at the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge "University of Cambridge") defined web3 as "the putative next generation of the web's technical, legal, and payments infrastructure—including blockchain, [smart contracts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smart_contract "Smart contract") and cryptocurrencies." [^27]

Some visions are based around the concept of [decentralized autonomous organizations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decentralized_autonomous_organization "Decentralized autonomous organization") (DAOs).[^28] [Decentralized finance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decentralized_finance "Decentralized finance") (DeFi) is another key concept; in it, users exchange currency without bank or government involvement.[^5] [Self-sovereign identity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-sovereign_identity "Self-sovereign identity") allows users to identify themselves without relying on an authentication system such as [OAuth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OAuth "OAuth"), in which a trusted party has to be reached in order to assess identity.[^29]

## Reception

Academic researchers, such as Tomer J. Chaffer and Justin Goldston in 2022, have described web3 as a possible solution to concerns about the over-centralization of the web in a few " [Big Tech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Tech "Big Tech") " companies.[^30] [^5] [^25] Some have expressed the notion that web3 could improve [data security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Data_security "Data security"), [scalability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scalability "Scalability"), and [privacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet_privacy "Internet privacy") beyond what is currently possible with [Web 2.0](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Web_2.0 "Web 2.0") platforms.[^31] *Bloomberg* states that skeptics say the idea "is a long way from proving its use beyond niche applications, many of them tools aimed at crypto traders".[^26] *[The New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times "The New York Times")* reported that several investors are betting $27billion that web3 "is the future of the internet".[^32] [^33]

Some Web 2.0 companies, including [Reddit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reddit "Reddit") and [Discord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discord_\(software\) "Discord (software)"), have explored incorporating web3 technologies into their platforms.[^5] [^34] On November 8, 2021, CEO Jason Citron tweeted a screenshot suggesting Discord might be exploring integrating cryptocurrency wallets into their platform. Two days later, and after heavy user backlash,[^34] [^35] Discord announced they had no plans to integrate such technologies and that it was an internal-only concept that had been developed in a company-wide [hackathon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hackathon "Hackathon").[^35]

Some legal scholars quoted by *[The Conversation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Conversation_\(website\) "The Conversation (website)")* have expressed concerns over the difficulty of regulating a decentralized web, which they reported might make it more difficult to prevent [cybercrime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cybercrime "Cybercrime"), [online harassment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyberbullying "Cyberbullying"), [hate speech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hate_speech "Hate speech"), and the dissemination of [child pornography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Child_pornography "Child pornography").[^36] But, the news website also states that, "\[decentralized web\] represents the cyber-libertarian views and hopes of the past that the internet can empower ordinary people by breaking down existing power structures". Some other critics of web3 see the concept as a part of a [cryptocurrency bubble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptocurrency_bubble "Cryptocurrency bubble"), or as an extension of [blockchain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blockchain "Blockchain") -based trends that they see as overhyped or harmful, particularly [NFTs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-fungible_token "Non-fungible token").[^34] Some critics have raised concerns about the [environmental impact of cryptocurrencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_effects_of_bitcoin "Environmental effects of bitcoin") and NFTs.[^6] Cryptocurrencies vary in efficiency, with [proof of stake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proof_of_stake "Proof of stake") having been designed to be less energy intensive than the more widely used [proof of work](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proof_of_work "Proof of work"), although there is disagreement about how secure and decentralized this is in practice.[^37] [^38] [^39] [^40] Others have expressed beliefs that web3 and the associated technologies are a [pyramid scheme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyramid_scheme "Pyramid scheme").[^6]

[Jack Dorsey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Dorsey "Jack Dorsey"), co-founder and former CEO of [Twitter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitter "Twitter"), dismissed web3 as a "venture capitalists' plaything".[^41] Dorsey opined that web3 will not democratize the internet, but it will shift [power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_\(social_and_political\) "Power (social and political)") from players like [Facebook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Facebook "Facebook") to venture capital funds like [Andreessen Horowitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andreessen_Horowitz "Andreessen Horowitz").[^9]

### Buzzword

Liam Proven, writing for *[The Register](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Register "The Register")*, concludes that web3 is "a myth, a fairy story. It's what parents tell their kids about at night if they want them to grow up to become economists".[^42]

In 2021, [SpaceX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SpaceX "SpaceX") and [Tesla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tesla,_Inc. "Tesla, Inc.") CEO [Elon Musk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elon_Musk "Elon Musk") expressed skepticism about web3 in a tweet, saying that web3 "seems more marketing buzzword than reality right now." [^11]

In November 2021, James Grimmelmann of [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University "Cornell University") referred to web3 as [vaporware](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaporware "Vaporware"), calling it "a promised future internet that fixes all the things people don't like about the current internet, even when it's contradictory." Grimmelmann also argued that moving the internet toward a blockchain-focused infrastructure would centralize and cause more [data collection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Data_collection "Data collection") compared to the current internet.[^10]

Software engineer Stephen Diehl described web3 in a blog post as a "vapid marketing campaign that attempts to reframe the public's negative associations of crypto assets into a false narrative about disruption of legacy tech company hegemony." [^43]

### Decentralization concerns

[Kevin Werbach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Werbach "Kevin Werbach"), author of *The Blockchain and the New Architecture of Trust*,[^44] has said that "many so-called 'Web 3.0' solutions are not as decentralized as they seem, while others have yet to show they are scalable, secure and accessible enough for the mass market", adding that this "may change, but it's not a given that all these limitations will be overcome".[^45]

In early 2022, [Moxie Marlinspike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moxie_Marlinspike "Moxie Marlinspike"), creator of [Signal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signal_\(messaging_app\) "Signal (messaging app)"), wrote about web3 as not being as decentralized as it appears to be, mainly due to consolidation in the cryptocurrency field, including in blockchain [application programming interfaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Application_programming_interfaces "Application programming interfaces") which are currently mainly controlled by the companies [Alchemy](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Nikil_Viswanathan&action=edit&redlink=1 "Nikil Viswanathan (page does not exist)") and [Infura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infura "Infura"); [cryptocurrency exchanges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptocurrency_exchanges "Cryptocurrency exchanges") which are mainly dominated by [Binance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binance "Binance"), [Coinbase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coinbase "Coinbase"), [MetaMask](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MetaMask "MetaMask"), and [OpenSea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OpenSea "OpenSea"); and the [stablecoin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stablecoin "Stablecoin") market which is currently dominated by [Tether](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tether_\(cryptocurrency\) "Tether (cryptocurrency)"). Marlinspike also remarked that the new web resembles the old web.[^46] [^47] [^48]
